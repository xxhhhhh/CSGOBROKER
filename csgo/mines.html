Mines (Minesweeper) - A popular gambling game mode inspired by the classic Minesweeper game, which has gained popularity on CS2 and CS:GO skin gambling sites. CS2 Mines Sites: How the Game Works and What to Compare

Mines is a random grid game with hidden mines. Before a round, users choose a wager and usually the number of mines, then reveal cells one by one. A safe cell increases the current multiplier, while hitting a mine ends the round and loses the wager.

The more mines selected and the more safe cells already revealed, the higher the potential multiplier and the lower the probability of continuing successfully. The core mathematics therefore depend on mine count, grid size, payout table, and house edge.

CSGOBROKER evaluates Mines separately from Dice, Crash, and Plinko. Important factors include Provably Fair, the multiplier table, cashout after safe picks, limits, and automation rules.

How CS2 Mines Works

The grid

The platform places hidden mines among closed cells. Grid size and supported mine counts can vary.

Choosing the mine count

Fewer mines increase the chance of a safe pick but produce slower multiplier growth. More mines raise both risk and potential payout.

Revealing safe cells

After each successful selection, the multiplier typically increases and the user can continue or cash out.

Hitting a mine

Selecting a mined cell ends the round. Previously revealed safe cells do not normally return the wager.

Automatic selection

Some sites can automate picks or a target number of steps. This changes convenience, not the probability of a particular layout.

Provably Fair

A verifiable system can reveal data after the round so the mine placement can be independently checked.

What to Check Before Playing Mines

Two visually identical grids can use different payout tables and different effective house edges.

Grid size and mine count

Review available configurations and how they affect the multiplier after each safe reveal.

Multiplier table

Compare actual payouts with the probability of surviving the corresponding number of picks.

House edge

Two sites can pay different multipliers for the same risk. Lower payouts at the same probability mean a larger platform edge.

Cashout rules

Check whether the round can be ended after any safe pick and whether a minimum multiplier applies.

Provably Fair

Verify that the mine layout of a completed round can be checked using published data.

Limits and bonuses

Minimum wager, maximum win, bonus wagering, KYC, and withdrawal rules can affect the practical payout.

Frequently Asked Questions About CS2 Mines

What is CS2 Mines?

A hidden-grid game where users reveal safe cells while avoiding mines.

How does the multiplier increase?

It generally rises after each safe cell, with the exact table depending on mine count and site rules.

Can I cash out before clearing the grid?

Usually yes after a safe pick, though the exact rules should be checked.

Do more mines mean a higher multiplier?

Generally yes because safe-pick probability is lower, but exact payouts vary.

Can mine positions be known in advance?

Not in a properly random game. Provably Fair verifies a completed layout rather than revealing it beforehand.

Does previous grid history help?

It can audit completed rounds but does not predict a fresh independent layout.

Can bonus balance be used?
